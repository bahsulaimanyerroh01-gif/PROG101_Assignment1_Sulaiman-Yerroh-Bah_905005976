# SDG, Open Source and Digital Public Goods

## 7.1 Sustainable Development Goal

**SDG 4: Quality Education.** The system supports Target 4.1, which aims for all children to complete primary and secondary education. Students cannot learn if they are often absent, and absence is usually the first sign of dropping out. By flagging students whose attendance rate falls below 90% (At risk) or 75% (Critical), the system helps schools act early.

## 7.2 Open Source Software

**Why it should be Open Source**
- Schools in Sierra Leone have small budgets, so a free system removes the cost barrier.
- Any school can read the logic, check that it is fair, and trust the results.
- The attendance limits (90 and 75) can be changed by each school or district.

**Benefits for Sierra Leone**
- No licence fees and no dependence on one company.
- Local developers and students can learn from the design and improve it.
- It can be adapted to local languages such as Krio and to local school calendars.
- Money saved can be spent on teaching materials.

**Potential community contributions**
- Developers: add features such as SMS alerts to parents, or an offline mode.
- Teachers: suggest better attendance limits and report problems.
- Universities and students: translate it, test it, and write documentation.
- Ministry of Education staff: share needs and feedback.

## 7.3 Digital Public Goods (DPGs)

**How it could become a Digital Public Good**
A Digital Public Good is open-source software, data or content that is freely available, adaptable, and made for public benefit. This system could qualify by using an open licence (MIT), publishing clear documentation, protecting student privacy, and being open for any school or country to reuse.

**Accessibility, openness and public benefit**
- Accessibility: it should be simple to use, work on low-cost computers and offline, and be available in local languages.
- Openness: the design, pseudocode and licence are public so others can copy and improve them.
- Public benefit: every school, regardless of size or location, can use it at no cost to track attendance and support students.

## 7.4 Sierra Leone Community Impact

**Problem being solved**
Students miss school because of poverty, distance, illness, early marriage or work. Paper registers make it hard to notice patterns, so help often comes too late.

**Expected impact on the local community**
- Teachers identify at-risk students sooner.
- Parents are informed early and can act.
- Fewer students drop out.
- Schools save time compared with checking registers by hand.

**Beneficiaries**
- Students, especially girls and children in rural areas
- Parents and guardians
- Teachers and head teachers
- The Ministry of Education and the wider community

**Long-term sustainability**
- Open-source code lets the community maintain the system.
- Training for teachers and school staff keeps it in use.
- Schools can adjust the limits to their own needs.
- Partnerships with the Ministry of Education, universities and NGOs can support updates and growth.
